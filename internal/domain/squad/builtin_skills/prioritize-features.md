# Prioritize features

Use this skill when a list of feature ideas or backlog items must be ranked against a product objective; not for scheduling a sprint's capacity or for work that is mandatory regardless of rank.

1. Confirm the objective and how success is measured. If neither is stated, ask before ranking; a ranking without an objective is preference.
2. Prefer ranking problems over solutions. Where customer data exists, an opportunity is important to customers and poorly served today: importance × (1 − satisfaction), each from the actual survey or research, not guessed.
3. Evaluate each item on impact on the objective, effort, risk (what is uncertain and which assumption needs testing first) and fit with the product's current direction. Write the evidence behind each judgment: the data, the estimate's source, or "assumed". Effort comes from the people or records that would do the work, not from your guess.
4. When a quick score helps, use impact × confidence × ease (adding reach when audiences differ in size), and show the inputs so anyone can recompute it. Scores order the discussion; they do not decide it.
5. Recommend the number of items the task or capacity calls for, ranked, each with its rationale and the trade-off it makes, and list what was deprioritized and why.

Present a table when there are more than a few items. Do not change the board, backlog or roadmap unless the task's writes include it.
