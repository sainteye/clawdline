# Conversion review

Use this skill when a marketing page or form converts poorly and you are asked why or what to change; not for running experiments, changing tracking or editing live pages without authorization.

1. Establish the page type, its one primary conversion, where traffic comes from, the current rate if measured, and what was already tried. Read the project's product and audience context first. A missing number stays unknown; do not estimate it.
2. Review in order of likely impact, citing what on the page shows each finding:
   - **Value proposition:** can a newcomer tell what this is and why it matters at a glance, in the customer's words, and does the headline match the message that brought them?
   - **Call to action:** one clear primary action, visible early, wording that says what the visitor gets; a deliberate secondary action; repeated at decision points.
   - **Hierarchy:** a skim conveys the main message; visuals support it.
   - **Trust:** real, attributed proof near the action and after each claim.
   - **Objections:** price, fit, effort and risk answered where they arise.
   - **Friction:** unneeded fields, unclear next steps, mobile problems, slow load.
3. For forms, every field must earn its place: ask what is needed before you can help, defer the rest, infer what can be inferred. Visible labels, not placeholders as labels; one column; easy fields first and sensitive ones last; inline errors next to the field that keep the input; a button that names what the person gets; a clear post-submit state; mobile keyboards and autofill. Any reassurance such as "we never share your data" must be true of the actual policy.
4. Separate what to change from what to test. A change is a likely fix with evidence; a test is a hypothesis with the metric it would move and why. Do not promise an uplift or quote generic benchmark numbers.

Deliver: quick fixes, larger changes in priority order, test hypotheses, and two or three alternatives for the headline and call to action. Measurement setup and experiments are proposals until authorized.
