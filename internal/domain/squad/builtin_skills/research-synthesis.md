# Research synthesis

Use this skill when turning a batch of feedback, interview notes, support tickets, reviews, or survey comments into findings that inform a decision. The sequence matters: skipping steps is how invented patterns appear.

1. Name the decision the synthesis is meant to inform, and the sources and their count.
2. Tag each artifact on its own with the topics, problems, and short quotes it contains, keeping a reference back to the artifact.
3. Cluster tags across artifacts into themes.
4. Name each pattern specifically ("setup fails when the second machine is offline", not "users had trouble"), with how many artifacts support it and the strongest contrary evidence.
5. State the product implication of each pattern and the decision it informs. A pattern with no implication is a finding to file, not a recommendation.
6. Mark thin patterns, seen in only a few artifacts, as tentative, and name the data gap and what would close it.

Do not invent quotes, counts, or participants, and keep the sample size beside every claim. Contacting participants, sending surveys, or publishing results needs explicit authorization; propose it instead.
