# Copywriting

Use this skill when you write or rewrite marketing copy for a page, a headline, a call to action or a value proposition; not for emails, sending anything, or a final de-AI polish of settled text.

1. Read what the project already says about its product, audience, voice and past tests (a product or brand context file, existing pages). Ask only for what is missing: the page's one primary action, who it is for, their problem in their own words, their objections, what differs from alternatives, the proof available, and where visitors arrive from.
2. Diagnose before drafting and state it in one line with evidence. **Blank or generic:** build the argument from the brief; free on angle, never on facts. **A real voice is present:** keep its argument, vocabulary and emphasis; fix the buried lead, vague lines and structure. **Point edit:** change only what was asked and the minimum around it; return the rest untouched.
3. Draft in units (headline, subhead, call to action, each section, proof lines): two or three candidates each, keep the best, fix its worst flaw, lock it, then read the whole page once for flow.
4. Gate every line: can the reader picture it; can it be shown true or false; could a competitor print the same sentence. Fewer than two passes means rewrite from the facts, or mark the gap `[NEED: …]`.
5. Claims must be supportable from the material given. When a requested claim is not, do not write it and do not drop it silently: name it, say why it falls short, offer the closest supportable line, and state the evidence that would unlock it. Never invent numbers, customers, quotes or stories.
6. Clarity over cleverness; benefits connected to features; specifics over adjectives; the customer's words over company words; one idea per section. Most lines plain, one or two with punch.
7. Before delivering, revert any change you cannot give a reason for. Remove patterns that read as generated: a contrast staged against something nobody said, lists of what the product is not, a claim trailed by a string of add-ons, a question that answers itself, stock openers and tidy closing slogans. Follow the language's own punctuation and the brand's register.

Deliver the copy by section with short notes on why, and two or three alternatives for the headline and call to action; for a point edit, only the changed lines with a reason each. Publishing or testing the copy is a separate, authorized step.
