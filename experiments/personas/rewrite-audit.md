# Persona rewrite audit

The 42 texts in `internal/domain/persona/catalog/` are rewrites of
[agency-agents](https://github.com/msitarzewski/agency-agents) at commit
`053ddbbf392a1688fc7043d81529f47ef2cf86c8`. Upstream personas carry brief-like content — default
verdicts, mandatory steps, automatic-fail triggers, report templates, success criteria — and the
question was whether our rewrites lost some of it by accident. One such loss was already known:
upstream's Evidence Collector rules PASS/FAIL per claim and defaults to FAILED, while ours said "Do
not render a verdict", and in round 3 that made both persona + vague runs withhold the merge verdict.

Each text was compared with the upstream file(s) its `source:` line names. Every difference was
classified:

- **Deliberate**: stack-specific content, emoji, organisational process (SLAs, training, leadership
  reporting), "memory" claims, unsourced metrics, marketing tone, long code samples, and anything
  that conflicts with Clawdline's protocol or the precedence preamble.
- **Accidental**: a rule reversed, a default softened, the role's key discipline or a mandatory step
  lost, or a deliverable shape lost that a Clawdline session needs.

Round 4 added one more test for every text: a rule that withholds a fix or a report for lack of
evidence ("no evidence, no report") made runs skip a correct constant-time compare. Wherever a text
demanded evidence that way, it now says to mark the item unverified and say why; cheap, clearly
correct hardening stays allowed. These lines are counted as accidental even where upstream had no
matching rule, because the rewrite introduced them.

Line numbers in the table refer to the texts before this audit's changes.

## Summary

- **Audited**: 42. **With accidental losses**: 29. **Fixed**: 29. **No accidental loss**: 13
  (`architect`, `content-writer`, `email`, `growth`, `image-prompt`, `incident-commander`,
  `instagram`, `pr`, `pricing`, `product-manager`, `social-media`, `trend-researcher`,
  `ux-architect`).
- **Most common kinds**, roughly in order:
  1. *Evidence phrased as refusal* (the round-4 pattern): a claim, number or finding that could
     not be confirmed was dropped instead of reported as unverified — `analytics`, `brand-guardian`,
     `code-reviewer`, `ai-search`, `devops`, `feedback-synthesizer`, `finops`, `minimal-change`,
     `performance`, `security`, `support`, `technical-writer`, `evidence-collector`.
  2. *Default verdict or default requirement softened or lost*: `evidence-collector` (reversed),
     `ui-finish-gate` (no default fail), `reality-checker`, `performance` (no meets/fails verdict),
     `test-automation` (no stability bar), `ui-designer` and `ui-finish-gate` (no WCAG AA
     threshold), `accessibility` (zoom, reduced motion and forced colours made conditional),
     `backend` (security-by-default lost).
  3. *Core discipline or mandatory step lost*: `ui-finish-gate` (the product lens and the
     "generic UI" blocker), `seo` (one page per query), `sre` (progressive rollout), `secrets`
     (credential overlap during rotation), `privacy` (lawful basis, cross-border transfer),
     `ux-researcher` (participant privacy, contradicting evidence), `frontend` (tests for new
     states), `devrel` (failure modes in tutorials), `customer-success` (early risk signals).
  4. *Deliverable shape lost*: `api-tester` (security failures first), `sprint-prioritizer` (a
     round goal with a checkable success condition), `code-reviewer` (why each finding matters).
- One rule was **reversed** rather than softened: `evidence-collector`. `finops` had a milder
  reversal (headroom a reliability target needs could be cut once confirmed; upstream treats
  reliability targets as constraints).
- Largest text after the changes: `ui-finish-gate`, 5,149 bytes injected (5,495 on disk); every
  text stays well under `persona.MaxPersonaBytes` (8 KiB injected).

## What round 5 should test

Rounds 1–4 compared "our rewrite vs none" and hit the ceiling: tasks the model already does well
leave no room for a persona to help. Round 5 should compare **upstream original vs our rewrite
(after this audit) vs none**, on tasks chosen below the ceiling — where a baseline run misses
something in at least some samples — and scored by held-out checks. The personas with the largest
restored content are the natural first subjects: `evidence-collector` (does a per-claim default-FAIL
verdict change merge decisions?), `ui-finish-gate`, `security` (does the defence-in-depth rule keep
the timing-safe fix without adding noise?), and `test-automation`. A pre-audit arm for the same
personas would show whether the audit itself changed anything.
