How I season a cast iron skillet in the oven

By Maya Lindqvist · Published March 14, 2026

I bought my 12-inch skillet at a yard sale for $8. It had rust spots on the bottom and a sticky, uneven layer on the cooking surface. These are the steps I used to strip it back and build up a new seasoning in my home oven, over one afternoon.

What you need

  • A cast iron skillet, scrubbed clean with steel wool and dried on the stove
  • A neutral oil with a high smoke point (I used canola)
  • Lint-free cloths or paper towels
  • An oven that reaches 450°F (230°C)

Step 1: Oil it thinly

With the pan still warm, rub a teaspoon of oil over every surface, including the handle and the bottom. Then wipe it all off again as if you had made a mistake. What stays behind is enough; a thick coat turns sticky.

Step 2: Bake it upside down

Put the skillet upside down on the middle rack, with a sheet of foil on the rack below to catch drips. Bake for one hour at 450°F (230°C), then turn the oven off and let the pan cool inside it.

Step 3: Repeat

I did three rounds. After the third, the surface was an even, dull black, and a fried egg slid around with a little butter. Your pan may need more or fewer rounds.

What went wrong the first time

On my first try I left too much oil on the pan and got brown, tacky spots. Scrubbing those off and starting again with a thinner coat fixed it.
